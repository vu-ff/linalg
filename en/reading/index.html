Reading

The books this course is built on, and what to read from them week by week. Nothing here is required: the lecture notes are self-contained, and the problems are set from them. Read around a lecture when you want a second voice on it — a different author's account of the same theorem is often what makes it land.

The two main texts

One abstract, one physics-facing. Between them they cover Parts I and II; if you buy nothing else, these are the two.

  • S. Axler Linear Algebra Done Right 4th ed.

    The abstract spine of Parts I and II. It treats vector spaces and operators without leaning on determinants, which is exactly the training that pays off later in quantum mechanics. One warning: Axler's inner product is linear in the FIRST slot, the opposite of the convention this course uses from Lecture 7 on. Where the notes quote him, the two slots have been swapped silently.

  • A. Papadopoulos & F. Scheck Linear Algebra for Physics

    The physics-facing parallel text: the same material in the language you will meet it in later. Dirac formalism, the spectral theorems and tensors. Read it alongside Axler rather than instead of him — where the two disagree in emphasis, that disagreement is the point.

Supplementary, by topic

Opened for a particular topic rather than read through. Each note says what the book is good for.

  • S. Treil Linear Algebra Done Wrong free online

    Free and abstract, with strong exercises. Best used for the dual space, tensors and a second account of the spectral theorem.

  • N. Johnston Advanced Linear and Matrix Algebra

    Explicit Kronecker products and index arrays. The book to open when the tensor product of Lecture 12 needs to become a matrix you can actually write down.

  • A. Lukas The Oxford Linear Algebra for Scientists

    Gentle and computational, with Dirac notation and physics examples from early on. The right book if a lecture's abstraction has outrun your intuition and you want the concrete version first.

  • A. I. Lvovsky Quantum Physics linear-algebra appendix, appa

    Linear algebra written entirely in bra-ket — the physicist's picture of everything Part I proves. Short, and worth reading once the inner product is in place.

  • S. Roman Advanced Linear Algebra

    A graduate reference for multilinear algebra. To consult on a specific question in Lectures 11–12, not to read through.

  • T. Kato Perturbation Theory for Linear Operators

    The standard reference behind the optional Lecture 15. To consult, not to read through.

  • J. J. Sakurai & J. Napolitano Modern Quantum Mechanics

    Time-independent perturbation theory — the physics-facing parallel to the Rayleigh–Schrödinger material of Lecture 15.

  • C. M. Bender & T. T. Wu Anharmonic Oscillator Physical Review 184 (1969), 1231–1260

    The primary source for the optional quartic-oscillator divergence and its accumulating complex branch points. A research paper, and reads like one.

Part III — infinite dimensions

The last two lectures leave finite dimensions behind, and the two main texts stop being enough.

  • B. Hall Quantum Theory for Mathematicians

    The reference for Part III: Hilbert spaces, bounded and unbounded operators, the spectral theorem, and the quantum mechanics that needs them. Lecture 13 leaves one proof to it explicitly.

  • S. Axler Measure, Integration & Real Analysis MIRA free online

    Optional analytic backup for L² and completeness. Use it sparingly: the course assumes little analysis, and this book assumes more than the course does.
